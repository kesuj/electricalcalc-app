Small off-grid home in Jumla (Karnali) - no NEA line

A family with no grid connection wants lights, a TV, phone charging, a laptop and Wi-Fi, and power for 2 days without sun. Jumla is cold (record low about −15 °C), so the battery lives indoors.

What they use

  • 6 × LED bulb (10 W) for 5 h a day
  • 1 × LED TV (32-43 inch) (60 W) for 4 h a day
  • 2 × Mobile phone charger (10 W) for 3 h a day
  • 1 × Laptop (60 W) for 3 h a day
  • 1 × Wi-Fi router (10 W) for 8 h a day

Total about 860 Wh a day (≈ 26 units a month).

What the app designed

  • 1 × 550 W panel on a metal frame tilted 29° facing south
  • MPPT charge controller (NEPQA-2025 allows PWM only below 20 A; MPPT harvests more in the cold)
  • LiFePO4 battery 24 V, about 89 Ah (2.1 kWh) with BMS - kept indoors above 5 °C
  • Pure-sine inverter of at least 280 W continuous (the smallest common size, 500 VA-1 kVA, is fine)
  • Fuse at the battery, DC isolator, DC surge protector, AC breaker + 30 mA RCD, 2 earth pits

Detailed wiring

  1. Panel → controller: the panel's MC4 leads go on 1 string of solar cable (4 mm² H1Z2Z2-K, red +, black −) down to a DC isolator and a DC surge protector, then into the MPPT controller's PV terminals. Cover the panel while wiring - it is live in daylight.
  2. Battery first: connect the controller's battery terminals to the battery through the battery fuse (fuse within 30 cm of the battery +) and isolator. Always connect the battery to the controller before the panel, and disconnect the panel first.
  3. Battery → inverter: short, thick cables (size in the cable table) from the same battery bus, through the fuse/isolator.
  4. Inverter → house: inverter AC output → AC breaker → 30 mA RCD → the house circuits (brown live, blue neutral, green-yellow earth).
  5. Earthing: panel frame, mounting frame, controller and inverter cases → earth bar → two earth pits (≤ 10 Ω, AEPC spec).

Detailed wiring - 500 kW

  1. Strings: 19 panels in series per string (≈ 790 V working, up to 1,000 V on the coldest morning - below the 1,100 V inverter limit); 48 strings in total, 4 mm² H1Z2Z2-K solar cable with genuine MC4s.
  2. Strings → inverters: up to 12 strings into each 100 kVA inverter's MPPT inputs, with gPV string fuses where 3+ strings share an input and a Type 2 DC SPD per inverter.
  3. Inverters → AC combiner: each inverter through its own breaker on 70 mm² copper (3-phase 400 V) to the LV panel next to the transformer.
  4. Step-up: LV panel → 500 kVA 0.8/11 kV ONAN transformer → 11 kV RMU/VCB with protection relays.
  5. To the grid: NEA revenue metering → 2 km 11 kV line → NEA substation. Earth mesh joins every frame, inverter, transformer neutral and fence.

Honest by design

Calculations are deterministic

Every number comes from a formula you can read in the report (Boxwell's Solar Electricity Handbook, Neill et al.'s Solar Farms, IEC standards) - never from AI guesses.

Sources and dates shown

Prices and rules carry their source and date. Assumptions are labelled. Data that could not be verified is said to be unverified.

Know the stop-points

The report tells you what NEA must approve, what is compulsory for safety, and when to get help with 230 V wiring.
